Day / Night — a page with 18 controls on it

Mockups for OpenIPC/majestic-webui#325. Nothing here is built yet — the point is to agree the shape before any of it reaches the source. Each round of feedback gets its own version so they stay comparable.

  • v3 — your Legacy switch, and nothing hidden

    Rebuilt from the second round of feedback. Interactive, and in the real page chrome with the settings rail, which the earlier two were not.

    • No collapsible sections and no second level of navigation. Every setting is on the page, grouped visually under plain headings.
    • Descriptions stay on the page — they are just shorter. 2,998 characters became 703, a 77% cut, and nothing sits behind a “?”. The wording still wants a human pass.
    • A single Legacy switch, off by default, is the only thing that hides anything — and it is a mode, not navigation.
    • The Dashboard notice for a camera upgraded from a version that only had the old settings.
    • Polarity chips stay on the wiring, from v2.

  • v2 — polarity belongs to the wiring

    Both “inverted” settings were bucketed with the older switching mechanisms. That was a category error: an invert is not a way of deciding day from night, it is a fact about how the board is wired — and the pin map already owns wiring.

    • Each assigned role carries a chip saying what its pad does in the active state — lamp on = HIGH, dark = HIGH, night = HIGH. Clicking flips it.
    • The word “inverted” leaves the page, and with it 375 characters of hint explaining when the setting applies.
    • The single-coil rule becomes visible: the IR-cut chip exists only while one coil drives the filter, which is the daemon's own rule rather than a paragraph about it.
    • The camera light gets one too — proposed, because it is the only pad majestic drives with no polarity setting today.
    • The lamp's pad is reachable from its own fold, which previously said “no lamp wired” and offered no way to wire it.

  • v1 — the section with almost nothing on it

    The reporter of #325 asked for two things: fewer words, and only the settings that are actually in use. Following that to its end takes most of the page away.

    • Three controls on the page instead of eighteen, plus the switch and its readout.
    • Three folds — tuning, camera light, older ways of switching — each stating its own condition rather than repeating a warning under every field.
    • The pin map is untouched. It is the part of this page that works.
    • A raw threshold appears only on a camera that already has one, shown with what it means on that sensor, and with a button out.
    • English and Russian, side by side — the section is fully localised so the Russian-speaking community can review the wording, not just the layout.
